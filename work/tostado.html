The brief

A neighborhood roaster on Calle Ocho with two very different customers: the regular who wants a cafecito in thirty seconds, and the coffee nerd who wants to know the farm.

The site had to work for both without making either one wait. It also had to sell what keeps a small roaster alive between rushes: bags of beans and subscriptions.

The idea

Everything is a circle seen from above: the cup, the logo, the roast-date stamp. It's what you see when someone hands you a coffee through the window.

The hero is a cup of café con leche with latte art that turns slowly, and a stamp that reads "Roasted Tuesday, brewed today". Freshness is the one claim that matters, so it's the first thing on the page and it's said plainly.

The ordering menu sits right below the hero on a dark espresso background. Regulars can add a cortadito in one tap without scrolling past a story they already know.

Color and type

The palette follows a cup from bottom to top: dark roast, the brown of a cortadito, terracotta from the tiles on 8th Street, and the cream of the espumita. A muted sage stands in for the plants outside the ventanita.

Challenges

Ordering in seconds

Each menu item is one row with a price and a single add button. The button turns into a check, the order counter in the header bounces, and a short message confirms it, so you always know it worked. The order opens in a side drawer you can close with Escape, and focus returns to where you were.

A subscription you can understand

Subscription forms usually hide the price until the last step. Here, every choice (coffee, grind, bag size, how often) updates a summary card beside the form, with the regular price crossed out next to the 10% subscriber price. Nothing is a surprise at checkout.

Telling the beans apart

Each bag card shows the origin, three tasting notes and a roast level drawn as five dots, with a text label so it doesn't depend on reading the dots. People who know coffee get the detail, everyone else gets "chocolate, molasses, almond".

The build

Hand-written HTML, CSS and a small amount of plain JavaScript for the menu tabs, the order drawer and the subscription builder. The menu tabs follow the ARIA tabs pattern with arrow-key support, the form uses real radio buttons styled as cards, and all animation stops for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

The page also uses the effects people expect from a modern brand site: beans that float at different parallax depths, headlines that rise in word by word, numbers that count up, magnetic buttons, cards that tilt toward the pointer, and a pinned "From farm to cup" section that scrolls sideways as you scroll down (on phones it becomes a swipeable row). All of it switches off for reduced motion.

For a real launch, this would connect to a store platform such as Shopify for checkout and recurring orders, with the same front end on top.
