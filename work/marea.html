The homepage on desktop. Open the live site to browse the schedule and book a class.

The brief

A boutique studio for reformer pilates, breathwork and recovery, in the busiest neighborhood in Miami. Its members come to slow down.

Fitness sites tend to shout: countdown timers, flashing offers, transformation photos. Marea's site had to do the opposite and still fill classes, because an empty reformer is lost money.

The idea

Marea means tide. The whole site moves at the pace of breathing: soft waves drifting at the bottom of the hero, light type, generous space.

Halfway down the page there's a small exercise instead of a sales pitch: a circle that grows and shrinks to guide three breaths. It's the fastest way to explain what the studio feels like, and it gives the visitor something before asking for anything.

The schedule is the real homepage for members, so it sits right after the hero, on a deep navy background that sets it apart from the rest of the page.

Color and type

Deep navy and tide teal from Biscayne Bay at dusk, sea foam and sand for the light sections, and a single coral accent saved for the moments that need attention: filters in use, few spots left, the most popular plan.

Breathe deeper.

Outfit Light for headlines. Rounded, open letters that feel calm even at large sizes.

Small classes, eight people at most.

Karla for text. A friendly grotesque that stays readable on the dark schedule.

Challenges

A schedule you can scan in one glance

Seven day buttons across the top, class type filters below, then one row per class: time, name and level, instructor, and how many spots are left, shown as text and a small bar that turns coral when only one or two remain. Full classes offer a waitlist instead of a dead end.

Booking without a new page

Booking opens a native dialog that confirms the class, time, instructor and the cancellation rule, then updates the row in place to "Booked" with one fewer spot. Focus handling, Escape to close and the dimmed background come from the browser's own dialog element.

Honest pricing

Three plans, a monthly or yearly switch, and the per-class cost written out under the price. The yearly view shows the total billed per year, so the discount never hides what you actually pay.

The build

Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript. The week's schedule is generated from a small set of class templates so every day looks different, the waves are three SVG layers drifting at different speeds, and every animation respects reduced-motion settings. On phones, each class row reflows into a compact card with the booking button always on the right.

A sticky "Your first class" section walks through the visit in four steps while a dial fills and its illustration changes beside the text; on phones the dial pins to the top of the screen. Headlines rise in word by word, the studio numbers count up, and the hero text drifts away slowly as you scroll, all switched off for reduced motion.

A real studio would connect this front end to its booking software, such as Mindbody, so members see live availability and can book from the same page.
