Tally: a dashboard that answers "how's the shop doing?" in five seconds.

The brief

Owners of small shops, a plant store, a bookshop, a bakery, check their numbers between customers, often on a phone, rarely for more than a minute.

Most analytics tools are built for analysts. Tally is built for the owner who wants three answers: am I selling more than last month, what's selling, and is anything about to run out.

The idea

One screen, read top to bottom, from the big picture to the details: four numbers, one trend, the top products, then the latest orders.

Every number comes with its comparison to the previous period and a direction arrow, so a change is never shown by color alone. The date range control at the top changes everything on the page at once, so there's never a mismatch between a chart and a number.

New shops see a short setup checklist at the top. It shows progress, can be dismissed, and disappears by itself once everything is done.

Color and type

A calm, cool base so the data stands out, and a single cobalt accent for everything interactive and for the charts. Green, amber and red are reserved for status (paid, pending, refunded) and always appear with an icon and a label. Dark mode uses its own adjusted colors rather than a simple inversion.

Challenges

Charts people can actually read

The revenue chart has one line, a soft fill and four gridlines on round numbers. Hovering shows a crosshair with the exact day, revenue and order count, and keyboard users can step through the days with the arrow keys while a screen reader reads each value. The top products list uses labeled bars rather than a pie chart, and flags low stock with a warning icon and text.

The same app on a phone

On small screens the sidebar becomes a bottom tab bar within thumb reach, the four numbers form a two-by-two grid, and the orders table turns into stacked cards so nothing needs sideways scrolling.

Settings without leaving the page

Settings open in a side drawer over the dashboard, so you can change the store name or turn on low-stock alerts and see the result immediately. Turning on alerts also checks off that step in the setup list.

The build

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no chart library: the charts are SVG drawn from the data, and they redraw when the panel is resized. Colors are design tokens, which is what makes the dark mode a single attribute change. The sample data is generated with a fixed seed so it looks realistic and stays the same between visits.

A production version would load real orders from an API, with the same components and states for loading, empty and error.
