Brasa: a restaurant website you can almost smell.

Project
Self-initiated concept
My role
Brand direction, UX and UI design, front-end build
Sector
Restaurants and hospitality
Deliverables
Homepage, menu, reservation flow, live site

The homepage on desktop. Open the live site to browse the menu and book a table.

The brief

A wood-fire restaurant where every dish touches a twelve-foot hearth. The site had to feel as warm and loud as the room, and still get people to a booked table quickly.

Most restaurant visitors come for three things: the menu, the hours and a reservation. Everything else on the site is there to make them want to come, not to slow them down.

The idea

Fire, used literally: the page opens on a near-black screen with a slow ember glow rising from the bottom and sparks drifting up through the name.

The name is set huge and heavy, half filled and half outlined, as if it's still catching. Below it, a ticker lists what's on the fire today, which tells regulars the menu changes and gives first-timers something to crave.

The voice is short and confident, with italic serif lines for the menu descriptions, the way a server would describe the dish at your table.

Color and type

Charcoal and ash for the room, ember orange and flame yellow for heat, and a warm bone white for the paper menus. The reservation section switches to bone, like turning the lights up when it's time to make a decision.

Challenges

A reservation in three taps

The booking flow asks for exactly three things, in order: party size, date and time. Days the restaurant is closed never appear, full time slots are shown crossed out instead of hidden, and the summary ticket on the side fills in as you go. The confirm button stays disabled until everything is chosen, and the confirmation shows a booking code straight away.

A menu that's easy to scan

Dishes sit in two columns on desktop with the price aligned on the same line as the name. Vegetarian and gluten-free tags are labeled in a legend, and a "vegetarian only" switch filters the list in place.

Booking on a phone

On phones, a slim bar at the bottom of the screen keeps "Book a table" one tap away while you read the menu, the same pattern I used for Palma Alta.

The build

Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript. The ember glow, sparks and ticker are CSS animations, and all of them stop for visitors who prefer reduced motion. Menu tabs support the arrow keys, and every booking option is a real button with its pressed state announced to screen readers.

On scroll, the giant name zooms and fades away, a warm glow follows the pointer across the hero, the kitchen numbers count up (including a countdown to zero gas burners), the menu tabs stay pinned while you read the dishes, and the Fire Nights stack on top of each other like cards on a table. Every effect stops for reduced motion.

For a real restaurant, the time slots would come from a reservation system such as OpenTable or Resy, and the menu from a simple content editor the kitchen can update daily.
